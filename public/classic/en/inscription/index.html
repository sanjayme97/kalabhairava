The Manakathuru Inscription

A glimpse of the Hoysala age

Date
Śaka 1022 · 1101 CE
Dynasty
Hoysala — Vishnuvardhana
Language & script
Old Kannada (Halegannada)
Lines
44
Original location
Near the old outlet (kodi) of the village tank
Published in
Epigraphia Carnatica, Vol. 5 (Hassan)

This inscribed stone is kept safe at the Sri Kalabhairaveshwara Temple today, but it is not related to this temple’s history — it is a record of Manakathuru village and of other temples of that time.

Overview

Dated Śaka 1022 (1101 CE), in the time of the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana, this inscription opens with a long eulogy of the king’s titles, and then offers priceless insights into the beliefs and the religious and social life of the community at the time.

It records major temple constructions by local leaders, the installation of deities and gifts of land. Above all, it names the people who founded Manakathuru — then called “Managatur”.

The stone

A tall slab with an arched top: a sculpted panel above, and below it rows of Old Kannada letters across the face of the stone. Today it stands on a black stone base within the temple premises.

Shiva linga
At the centre of the panel — the gifts recorded are to Shiva temples.
Seated worshipper
On the left, a figure seated in worship — perhaps the donor.
Cow and calf
On the right — a motif often carved on stones recording gifts.
Sun and moon
Above — the customary sign that the gift should last “as long as the sun and moon”.
The arched line
The first line of the inscription runs along the rim of the arch, just as the source document notes.

The letters on the lower part of the stone have worn with time. Please help preserve it — avoid touching the stone or applying turmeric, kumkum or oil, which slowly wear the letters away.

The king’s titles

The first part of the record (lines 3–18) praises the Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana with a garland of titles. Some of them:

  • Yadukula-kuvalaya-sudhakaraThe moon to the lotus of the Yadu race
  • Satya-ratnakaraAn ocean of truth
  • Dvaravatipura-varadhishvaraSupreme lord of Dvaravati (Belur / Halebidu)
  • Shaurya-mrigarajaA lion in valour
  • Talakadugonda-gandaThe hero who conquered Talakad
  • Vasantikadevi-labdha-varaprasadaBlessed with the grace of the goddess Vasantika

He is said to rule Gangavadi 96,000, Nolambavadi 32,000 and Hanungal 500, “punishing the wicked and protecting the good”.

People in the record

After the royal eulogy, the inscription turns to the people of our village.

Chama-gavunda

A mahaprabhu (chief). Founded Managatur, built the Adalagatta and installed the god Chaveshvara.

Lines 20–21

Sanka-gavunda & Chatta-gavunda

Also said to have built Managatur. Chatta-gavunda built the Chaveshvara temple, placed its kalasa and renewed the earlier grants.

Lines 21–24

Isanya-panditadeva

Son of Karttara-jiya. Built the Moolasthana temple and set up its kalasa.

Line 1

Karttara-jiya

Praised for homa, discipline, japa, meditation and virtue; the receiver of the grants.

Lines 1, 34

Masanoja

A carpenter, son of Chikkoja. Installed the god Chikkeshvara and made gifts.

Lines 25–26

Kalimayya & Maroja

Kalimayya, the senabova (village accountant), composed the record; Maroja carved it in stone.

Line 43

The date

The inscription is dated Śaka 1022, in the year named Vikrama, on the fifth day of the bright half of Phalguna, a Monday. This corresponds to 5 February 1101 CE — which by calculation fell on a Tuesday.

The record also mentions the year Yuva, which likely corresponds to 1095 CE.

Śaka 1022
Shalivahana Śaka era — add about 78 to get the CE year
Vikrama
The name of that year in the 60-year samvatsara cycle
Phalguna śuddha pañcamī
Fifth day of the bright fortnight of Phalguna
Somavara
Monday, as stated in the record
A note for history readers.Historians generally date Vishnuvardhana’s reign from about 1108 CE and his capture of Talakad to about 1116. The title Talakadugonda in a record dated 1101 is therefore noteworthy; in such cases scholars discuss whether the record was engraved later or the date read differently. This page gives the date exactly as the inscription states it.

The grants

Wet and dry land was given for the deities’ worship (anga-bhoga), bathing (abhisheka), food offerings and perpetual lamps. The plots are located by village landmarks — below the big tank, below the Adalagatta, in front of the temple, by the stream, to the south (tenkana) and west (haduvana) of the village.

For Chaveshvara
Wet land below the big tank and below the Adalagatta; an ox-driven oil press to keep the perpetual lamp burning.
For Moolasthana
Wet land in front of the temple, black-soil wet land and dry land.
For Chikkeshvara
Wet land below the big tank and dry land.
For the Brahmins of Brahmapuri
Wet land and dry land.
For Sankimoja
Ten kolaga of wet land and a hundred kamba of dry land.
For the gaudas
Kodangi wet land below the big tank, gavudagatta and dry land.

The gifts were made by washing the feet of Karttara-jiya and pouring water (dhārāpūrvaka). The record closes with the customary blessing and curse: whoever protects this gift earns the merit of giving a thousand kapila cows at the Ganga, Kurukshetra and Varanasi; whoever destroys it incurs the sin of killing those cows and Brahmins. The Sanskrit verse “svadattām paradattām vā…” warns that one who takes away land given by himself or by others is born a worm for sixty thousand years.

Some words explained

salage · ಸಲಗೆ
¼ mana — a grain measure; wet land is measured by the seed it takes. (from the source document)
mattar · ಮತ್ತರು
A unit for measuring land. (from the source document)
Dvaravatipura · ದ್ವಾರವತೀಪುರ
Belur or Halebidu — the Hoysala capital. (from the source document)
gadde / beddalu · ಗದ್ದೆ / ಬೆದ್ದಲು
gadde = wet (irrigated) land; beddalu = dry, rain-fed land.
nandadivige · ನಂದಾದೀವಿಗೆ
A perpetual lamp kept burning before the deity.
gavunda / gauda · ಗಾವುಂಡ / ಗೌಡ
Village headman.
senabova · ಸೇನಬೋವ
Village accountant and record-keeper (later shanubhoga).
kalasa · ಕಳಸ
The sacred pot-shaped finial set on top of a temple.
dharapurvaka · ಧಾರಾಪೂರ್ವಕ
Giving with a pouring of water; “kalam kacchi” = after washing the receiver’s feet.

The stone’s journey

The stone is known to have stood near the old outlet (kodi) of the Manakathuru tank. In the late 1960s or the 1970s the villagers brought it to the temple to keep it safe. It may once have belonged to an old temple that stood near the tank.

  1. c. 1095 CEThe year Yuva, also mentioned in the record.
  2. 1101 CEŚaka 1022, Phalguna śuddha pañcamī — the date of the inscription.
  3. 1902B. L. Rice publishes Epigraphia Carnatica, Vol. 5 (Hassan District), which records this inscription.
  4. 1960s–70sVillagers move the stone from near the old outlet (kodi) of the Manakathuru tank to the temple for safekeeping.
  5. TodayKept safe at the Sri Kalabhairaveshwara Temple.

Sources

  • B. L. Rice (ed.), Epigraphia Carnatica, Vol. 5 — Hassan District. The core information about this inscription comes from this volume.
  • Village document: “ಮನಕತ್ತೂರಿನ ಕಲ್ಬರಹ: ಹೊಯ್ಸಳ ಕಾಲದ ಒಂದು ನೋಟ” (The Manakathuru Inscription: A Glimpse of the Hoysala Age), 2025.
