Manakathuru

Sri Kalabhairaveshwara Swamy Temple

Sri Kalabhairaveshwara Swamy, the presiding deity of Manakathuru, at the heart of village life.

Sri Kalabhairava

The Shiva Purana and other Puranas describe Kalabhairava as the fierce form of Lord Shiva. In a well-known story, Bhairava emerges from Shiva himself to humble pride and ego.

Kāla means both time and death; as master of both he is Kalabhairava. By tradition he is worshipped as the kshetrapala who guards every Shiva shrine, and as the protector of the village.

Across the old Mysore region — especially around Hassan, Mandya and Tumakuru — many families worship Sri Kalabhairaveshwara as their family deity (manedevaru).

His form

Trishula & damaru

Symbols of Shiva’s power and of the primal sound of creation.

Kshetrapala

The temple at Manakathuru

Sri Kalabhairaveshwara Swamy Temple is the centre of Manakathuru’s religious and social life. Its entrance tower (gopura) is adorned with colourful sculptures of deities.

On festival days the deity is adorned with a silver prabhavali (arch) and elaborate floral alankara, and on jatra nights the whole temple glows with thousands of lights.

Manakathuru village

Manakathuru is a village in Arsikere Taluk, Hassan District. Its history goes back at least nine centuries — a Hoysala inscription of 1101 CE names the gavundas (headmen) who founded “Managatur”.

That inscription is kept safe at our temple today, but it is not related to this temple’s history. See the inscription page →
